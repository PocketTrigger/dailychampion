DAILY CHAMPION - HOW THE SITE WORKS
====================================

A fan-made daily Pokémon battle game in the style of "Daily Red". Each day everyone faces the same
boss trainer, drafts 6 Pokémon from the same pool of 9, and gets one official attempt. Everything
runs in the browser. There is no server, no account and no database.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE DAILY CYCLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- A "day" is the player's LOCAL calendar date, so the new boss unlocks at local midnight. The
  result screen shows a countdown and a preview of tomorrow's boss.
- Day #1 is 26 Oct 2026. The day number counts up from there.
- Boss choice: the 187 bosses are put in a shuffled order, seeded so it's the same for everyone,
  and one is used per day. After all 187 have appeared, a new shuffled order starts. So no boss
  repeats until every boss has had a turn.
- Everything "random" that must match between players (boss order, draft pool) uses a seeded
  random number generator (mulberry32, seeded by a hash of the date text). Battles themselves use
  normal randomness (damage rolls, crits, misses).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DRAFT POOL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each day has one pool of 9 Pokémon, shared by Normal and Hard. The player picks 6, and pick order
is party order (the first pick leads).

Who can appear:
- Base stat total (BST) of 470 or more. Huge Power and Pure Power users are the exception: they
  only need BST 380 or more (for example Azumarill, Medicham, Diggersby).
- Must have at least one attacking move.
- Restricted legendaries and mythicals ("tier L") may appear ONLY when the boss's team contains a
  legendary or sub-legendary, and at most 1 per pool.
- Sub-legendaries, Paradox Pokémon and Ultra Beasts ("tier S"): at most 2 per pool.
- The game prefers 9 different primary types, so the pool has variety.
- Alternate forms can appear like any other Pokémon, but a pool never holds two forms of the same
  species (for example Ninetales and Alolan Ninetales).

How the pool is balanced around the boss:
1. A quick "duel" estimate is computed for each pool Pokémon against each boss Pokémon. Both are
   built at level 100 with their Hard-mode movesets. For each side it works out how many hits its
   best move needs to KO the other. That uses the real damage formula, so it includes types, STAB,
   abilities and accuracy. Fewer hits wins, and on a tie the faster Pokémon wins (Truant counts as
   acting every other turn). The result is win, even or loss.
2. A candidate pool is accepted only if ALL of these hold:
   - every boss Pokémon is beaten by at least 2 of the 9 (an even result counts as half);
   - the boss's ace (last team slot) has at least 2 outright counters;
   - no single boss Pokémon beats 7 or more of the 9;
   - it isn't a walkover: at least one boss Pokémon has 4 or fewer counters, and the pool wins no
     more than 70% of all matchups.
3. Up to 300 seeded shuffles are tried and the first fair one is used. If none pass, the pool that
   breaks the rules by the least is used. The result is the same for everyone on that date.
4. The draft screen shows a one-line summary ("Every one of X's Pokémon has at least 2 counters in
   this pool") without revealing which Pokémon they are.

Testing: over a sample year (from 28 Oct 2026), 357 of 365 daily pools passed every rule. The
other 8 miss by the smallest possible amount (a Dragon-type boss's ace has 1 outright counter
instead of 2). Remember that the duel
estimate ignores healing, status, switching and stat boosts, so "fair" does not mean "easy".

Once a day's pool is generated it's saved with the player's run, so later changes to the
algorithm don't change a day already in progress. Practice battles get their own random,
boss-aware pool.

4. BATTLE ENGINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Format: 6 v 6 singles, turn based, Generation 9 rules (the same rules Pokémon Showdown uses).
Both sides are level 100. The player may switch, which uses their turn, and switches always
happen before moves.

Stats: every Pokémon has 31 IVs, 85 EVs in each stat and a neutral nature (similar to Showdown's
random battles). HP = 2*Base + 162. Other stats = 2*Base + 57 (at level 100).
On Hard, the BOSS's Pokémon are competitively trained instead (a "TRAINED" tag shows in its HP
box): 252 EVs in its main attacking stat and either Speed (fast attackers) or HP (slow or bulky
ones), with a nature that raises that attacking stat by 10% and lowers an unused stat. Trick Room
and Gyro Ball users lower Speed instead. Your team, Normal mode and the draft pool are unaffected.

Damage formula (Gen 9, as in the games):
  base = floor(floor(floor(2*Level/5 + 2) * Power * Atk / Def) / 50) + 2
  then, in the games' order: weather, critical hit (x1.5), one of 16 random rolls (85% to 100%),
  STAB (1.5, or 2 with Adaptability), type effectiveness, burn (x0.5 on physical moves, except
  Facade), screens, then ability and other modifiers. Modifiers are combined in 4096ths and
  rounded the way the games do it, so the numbers match Showdown's damage calculator.

Turn order: priority bracket first, then Speed (paralysis halves it, and Tailwind, weather,
abilities and stat stages also apply). Trick Room reverses Speed order inside each priority
bracket for 5 turns. Exact ties are random. Stall and Mycelium Might act last in their bracket.

Critical hits: stage 0 = 1/24, +1 = 1/8, +2 = 1/2, +3 or more = always. High-crit moves, Focus
Energy (+2) and Super Luck (+1) add stages. Crits ignore the attacker's negative stat stages, the
defender's positive ones, and screens. Battle Armor and Shell Armor block them.

Stat stages: -6 to +6 for Attack, Defense, Sp. Atk, Sp. Def and Speed (x(2+n)/2 or x2/(2-n)),
and for accuracy and evasion (x(3+n)/3 or x3/(3-n)). They reset on switching out (Baton Pass
keeps them).

PP: real PP values with the maximum PP Ups (x8/5), shown on the move buttons. Pressure makes
moves aimed at its user cost 2 PP. A Pokémon with no usable moves left uses Struggle (typeless,
loses 1/4 of its max HP each use).

Status conditions:
  Burn        1/16 HP per turn, physical damage halved
  Poison      1/8 HP per turn
  Toxic       1/16, 2/16, 3/16... per turn (the counter resets on switch-out)
  Paralysis   Speed halved, 25% chance to be unable to move
  Sleep       1-3 turns (Rest: exactly 2 turns)
  Freeze      20% chance to thaw each turn; some Fire moves thaw the user or the target
  Type immunities apply (Fire can't be burned, Electric can't be paralyzed, Poison and Steel can't
  be poisoned, Ice can't be frozen, Grass is immune to powder moves), plus ability, terrain,
  Safeguard and Substitute immunities.

Volatile states (cleared on switching out, shown as small tags under the HP bar):
  Confusion (2-5 turns, 1/3 chance to hit itself with a 40-power typeless physical attack),
  flinching (only if the attacker moved first), Substitute, Leech Seed, Taunt, Encore, Disable,
  Yawn, Perish Song, Destiny Bond, Focus Energy, Aqua Ring, Ingrain, Curse (Ghost type), Salt
  Cure, partial trapping (Fire Spin, Whirlpool... 4-5 turns), can't-escape trapping, Smack Down.

Every move's full effect list comes from Showdown's move data, including:
- Every secondary chance, rolled separately (Shadow Ball's 20% Sp. Def drop, Iron Head's 30%
  flinch, Scald's 30% burn...). Serene Grace doubles the chance; Sheer Force removes the effect
  and adds 30% power; Shield Dust and Substitute block them.
- Real contact flags, so Earthquake no longer triggers Rough Skin, but U-turn does.
- Multi-hit moves: 2-5 hits (35/35/15/15%, or always 5 with Skill Link), fixed 2- and 3-hit moves,
  Triple Axel's rising power, Population Bomb checking accuracy each hit. Every hit rolls its own
  critical hit and triggers contact abilities.
- Two-turn moves (Solar Beam, Meteor Beam, Electro Shot...), with Fly, Dig, Dive, Bounce, Phantom
  Force and Shadow Force making the user untouchable for that turn except to the moves that can
  hit it (Thunder vs Fly, Earthquake vs Dig, Surf vs Dive). Recharge moves (Hyper Beam...) skip
  the user's next turn. Outrage, Petal Dance and Thrash lock the user in for 2-3 turns, then
  confuse it.
- Fixed damage (Seismic Toss, Night Shade, Super Fang, Ruination, Endeavor, Final Gambit), damage
  returned (Counter, Mirror Coat, Metal Burst, Comeuppance), one-hit KO moves (Gen 9 accuracy,
  Sturdy blocks them) and self-KO moves (Explosion, Memento, Healing Wish...; Damp blocks
  Explosion-type moves).
- Variable power: Gyro Ball, Electro Ball, Heavy Slam, Heat Crash, Low Kick, Grass Knot,
  Eruption, Water Spout, Reversal, Flail, Hex, Venoshock, Stored Power, Weather Ball, Terrain
  Pulse, Body Press, Foul Play, Facade, Knock Off, Bolt Beak, Fishious Rend, Avalanche, Payback,
  Last Respects, Rage Fist and more.
- Conditions: Fake Out and First Impression only on the first turn after coming in, Sucker Punch
  and Thunderclap fail if the target isn't attacking, Upper Hand, Focus Punch (fails if hit
  first), Pursuit (doubles on a switching target), Dream Eater, Snore and Sleep Talk.
- Protect and its variants (Detect, King's Shield, Spiky Shield, Baneful Bunker, Silk Trap,
  Obstruct, Burning Bulwark) and Endure, with the games' 1/3 chance for each repeated use.
  Feint-type moves break protection.
- Accuracy and evasion: stages, weather (Thunder, Hurricane, Blizzard), No Guard, Compound Eyes,
  Hustle, Keen Eye, Sand Veil, Snow Cloak, Tangled Feet, Wonder Skin. Moves with "--" accuracy
  never miss. High Jump Kick and Supercell Slam crash on a miss.

Side conditions (shown in a strip on each side of the arena):
- Stealth Rock (Rock-type damage on entry: 1/8 neutral, up to 1/2 for 4x weak Pokémon), Spikes
  (up to 3 layers), Toxic Spikes (up to 2; a grounded Poison type removes them), Sticky Web.
  Flying types, Levitate and Magic Guard avoid the ones they should.
- Reflect, Light Screen, Aurora Veil (snow only), 5 turns. Brick Break, Psychic Fangs and Raging
  Bull break them. Infiltrator ignores them and Substitute.
- Tailwind (4 turns), Safeguard, Mist, Wish, Healing Wish / Lunar Dance.
- Rapid Spin, Mortal Spin, Defog and Tidy Up clear hazards; Court Change swaps both sides' effects.
- Future Sight and Doom Desire hit 2 turns later.

Switching moves: U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn, Parting Shot, Teleport, Chilly Reception, Baton
Pass and Shed Tail switch the user out mid-turn. The player picks who comes in from a prompt; the
boss picks with its normal switch logic. Roar, Whirlwind, Dragon Tail and Circle Throw drag in a
random foe (Suction Cups and Ingrain prevent it).

Other status moves: Rest, Pain Split, Haze, Clear Smog, Belly Drum, Fillet Away, Clangorous Soul,
No Retreat, Psych Up, Heal Bell, Aromatherapy, Strength Sap, Transform, Synthesis / Moonlight /
Morning Sun (heal more in sun, less in other weather), Shore Up (more in sand), Roost (loses
Flying type for the turn), Trick Room, weather and terrain moves, and every stat-changing move.

If both sides lose their last Pokémon at the same moment, the side that caused it (Explosion,
Destiny Bond, Final Gambit...) loses, as in the games.

Hard mode difference: every stat-boosting setup move (Swords Dance, Dragon Dance, Nasty Plot,
Calm Mind, Shell Smash, etc.) is replaced on EVERY Pokémon, the player's and the boss's, with a
Pokémon-specific attacking move from the HARD_MOVES table in data.js (or one generated by the
build script).

What is still NOT modelled:
- Held items (except Pikachu's Light Ball, which is built in). So Knock Off never gets its item
  bonus, Acrobatics always has its doubled 110 power, and Trick, Switcheroo, Fling and similar
  item moves are not used. Return uses its maximum-friendship power (102), as Showdown does.
- Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax and Terastallization (so Tera Blast is never used).
- Doubles-only effects, infatuation (Attract), and a handful of rarely seen moves that depend on
  them (Showdown marks these, and the build script never puts them in a set).
- Cosmetic and battle-only forms (Vivillon patterns, Mega Evolutions, Gigantamax...), Pumpkaboo
  and Gourgeist sizes, and Arceus and Silvally type forms. Forms that change during battle through
  an ability (Zen Mode, Stance Change, Schooling...) are handled by that ability.

5. ABILITIES, WEATHER AND TERRAIN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
About 245 abilities are implemented, and 1,094 of the 1,123 Pokémon and forms have a working
ability. Each Pokémon gets exactly one ability. The other 29 only have abilities that do nothing in this game
(item, doubles, Tera or info-only abilities such as Pickup, Cheek Pouch, Illusion, Frisk), so
they have none. Hover an ability in the game to see its description (ABILITY_INFO in
data.js).

Groups that are modelled:
- Entry effects: Intimidate, Download, Trace, Imposter, Intrepid Sword, Slow Start, Neutralizing
  Gas, weather and terrain setters.
- Damage boosts and reductions: Huge Power, Adaptability, Technician, Sheer Force, Tough Claws,
  the "-ate" abilities, Multiscale, Filter, Thick Fat, Fur Coat, Ruin abilities, Auras and more.
- Immunities and absorption: Levitate, Water/Volt Absorb, Flash Fire, Sap Sipper, Bulletproof,
  Soundproof, Wonder Guard, Good as Gold, Magic Bounce and more.
- Reactions to being hit: Rough Skin, Static, Justified, Weak Armor, Anger Point, Berserk, etc.
- Form changes: Stance Change, Schooling, Shields Down, Zen Mode, Power Construct, Zero to Hero,
  Forecast, Disguise, Ice Face.
- Trapping: Shadow Tag, Arena Trap and Magnet Pull. The player's switch buttons are disabled with
  a note, and the boss can't switch either.
- Mold Breaker, Teravolt and Turboblaze ignore the defender's ability.
- Abilities tied to the full move rules: Inner Focus and Steadfast (flinching), Own Tempo and
  Tangled Feet (confusion), Pressure (PP), Skill Link, Serene Grace, Shield Dust, Stench, Cursed
  Body, Infiltrator, Damp, Keen Eye, Compound Eyes, Suction Cups, Unseen Fist, Perish Body,
  Oblivious and Aroma Veil (Taunt and friends), and Magic Bounce reflecting hazards and status.

Weather (rain, harsh sun, sandstorm, snow) lasts 5 turns:
  Rain: Water x1.5, Fire x0.5, Thunder and Hurricane never miss. Sun: the reverse, and Thunder and
  Hurricane drop to 50% accuracy. Sand: 1/16 damage per turn to anything that isn't Rock, Ground
  or Steel, and Rock types get x1.5 Sp. Def. Snow: Ice types get x1.5 Defense, Blizzard never
  misses. Cloud Nine and Air Lock cancel weather while on the field.

Terrain (Electric, Grassy, Psychic, Misty) lasts 5 turns and affects grounded Pokémon (not Flying
types or Levitate users):
  Electric: Electric moves x1.3, no sleep. Grassy: Grass moves x1.3, heals 1/16 each turn,
  Earthquake halved. Psychic: Psychic moves x1.3, priority moves can't hit grounded targets.
  Misty: Dragon damage halved, no status conditions.

6. BOSS BEHAVIOUR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The boss makes its own decisions using the rules below. The player controls their own team.

Shared helpers:
- "Expected damage" of a move = the damage formula at an average roll (0.925) x accuracy,
  including abilities. A move that would be absorbed (for example a Water move into Water Absorb)
  scores 0.
- The boss can see the player's active Pokémon, its moves and ability, and its HP.

NORMAL
- 30% of the time it picks a completely random move. That's the "slips up" part.
- Otherwise it scores each move and picks the best:
  - Attacks: expected damage as a fraction of the target's remaining HP. A move that KOs gets a
    large bonus, and priority moves get extra when the boss is slower or about to be KO'd.
    Recoil and self-lowering moves are slightly discouraged.
  - Healing: used when below ~45% HP and not about to be KO'd.
  - Status moves: used if the target has no status and isn't immune (sleep rated highest).
  - Setup moves: used at high HP when the player isn't threatening much.
  - Support moves get simple rules: hazards while the player has 3+ Pokémon left, screens,
    Protect when the player is poisoned or seeded, Substitute at high HP, and so on (the same
    rules as Hard, listed below).
  - Its random picks skip moves that would do nothing (for example Seismic Toss into a Ghost).
- It never switches voluntarily, except through moves like U-turn. When a Pokémon faints it
  sends out the next one in team order.

HARD
- Trained stats for the boss's Pokémon (section 4).
- Attacks are chosen by scoring rules that predict the player will use their strongest move on
  the boss:
  - a guaranteed KO (even on a minimum damage roll) scores highest, a likely KO next, a 2HKO
    gets a small bonus;
  - if the player will move first AND KO the boss, its attacks are nearly worthless, so it
    looks for priority, Protect or a switch;
  - recoil at low HP, crash risk, recharge and self-KO moves are discouraged unless they KO.
  Rules for special moves still apply: Fake Out only on the first turn, no Dream Eater on an
  awake target, no Focus Punch into an attack, contact moves into Rough Skin discouraged, and
  multi-hit moves preferred against Substitute.
- Thinks one turn ahead about switching. A quick simulation plays out the turn for each option
  against the player's likely replies: their strongest move (weighted 70%), their second
  strongest (10%), and their best switch-in when the boss threatens a KO (20%). The simulation
  uses expected damage, priority and Speed, KOs, recoil, drain and Protect. It scores the position
  by HP left on each side, KOs, who wins the next exchange between the active Pokémon, and the
  free switch-in a side gets after a faint. The score is 80% the weighted average and 20% the
  worst case.
- It switches out directly only when the simulation says the switch is clearly better than its
  best attack (by 0.5, roughly half a Pokémon's HP). It won't switch more than twice in a row and
  can't switch while trapped.
- Switching attacks (U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn) get a bonus of at most 0.3 for exactly what
  the switch adds in the simulation. That's often nothing, so U-turn is only picked over a
  stronger attack when bringing in a teammate really helps. Switching status moves (Parting Shot,
  Teleport...) are only used when the switch is worth more than 0.15. Right after coming in by a
  switching move it strongly avoids using another one. Mid-turn, the teammate it brings in is
  the one that takes the player's predicted hit best and threatens back.
- Lead and replacements after a faint: a teammate that outspeeds and KOs the player's active
  Pokémon comes first (a revenge kill); otherwise the best matchup after hazard damage. The ace
  (last slot) is held back while another teammate can do the job.
- Perish Song: switches out on the last count when it can.
- Testing: a scripted player (drafts the best matchups from the pool, uses its strongest move,
  switches out of predicted KOs) played the same 120 Hard battles before and after this version.
  The boss won 39% before and 68% after (42% with the new decisions but untrained stats). Boss
  U-turn style moves fell from 0.61 to about 0.46 per battle, and it stopped bouncing straight
  back out on the next turn (8 times before, 0-2 after).
- Support and tactical moves (both difficulties):
  - Stealth Rock, Spikes, Toxic Spikes and Sticky Web while the player has 3+ Pokémon left;
    Defog, Rapid Spin, Court Change or Tidy Up when hazards are on the boss's side;
  - Reflect or Light Screen to match the player's stronger attacking stat, Tailwind or Trick Room
    when the boss's team is slower;
  - Protect when the player is taking poison, Leech Seed or Perish damage, to wait out a Wish, or
    to gain a Speed Boost; never twice in a row;
  - Substitute at high HP when the player can't hit hard; Taunt on Pokémon with 2+ status moves;
    Encore when the player just used a status move;
  - Roar, Whirlwind or Haze against a boosted Pokémon; Destiny Bond, Memento or Explosion when
    it is about to be KO'd anyway; Pain Split when the player has much more HP;
  - Rest and Sleep Talk together, Wish at mid HP, Heal Bell when teammates are statused.
- Both difficulties respect PP, recharge turns, charging moves, Outrage lock-in, Taunt, Encore
  and Disable. Fake Out is only chosen on the first turn.
- Hard also hides the "super effective" hints on the player's move buttons, and every setup move
  is swapped for an attack (section 4).

7. WHERE THE POKÉMON AND MOVE DATA COME FROM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sources:

A) Hand-curated (data.js, ~260 Pokémon)
   Originally written by hand: base stats, types, and
   4-move sets modelled on common competitive sets. These cover all boss Pokémon and many popular
   picks. They have since been CHECKED AGAINST SHOWDOWN'S DATA with tools/verify-data.js, and every
   mismatch was corrected: 2 base stats, 17 moves a Pokémon couldn't legally learn, 6 illegal
   Hard-mode swap moves, 2 abilities, and 3 move numbers. Base stats, types, abilities and move
   legality now all match Showdown. Since the battle-accuracy update, the 200 curated Pokémon
   that have a Showdown random-battle set use a set picked from it instead (see B), so signature
   moves are back (Rock Blast and Icicle Spear for Cloyster, Leech Seed and Gyro Ball for
   Ferrothorn, U-turn for Scizor...). The rest keep their hand-picked sets.

B) Generated (moves-gen.js with every move, and dex-all.js with the remaining ~767 Pokémon)
   Built by tools/build-dex.js from Pokémon Showdown's public data files:
     https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/data/pokedex.json   (species, base stats, types, abilities, tags)
     https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/data/moves.json     (move power, accuracy, type, effects)
     https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/data/learnsets.json (which moves each Pokémon can learn)
     smogon/pokemon-showdown repo: data/random-battles/gen9/sets.json (random-battle movepools)
   For each species not already hand-curated, the script:
   - converts EVERY move into moves-gen.js with its full effect data: secondary chances, contact
     and other flags, multi-hit, charge, recharge, lock-in, drain, recoil, crit ratio, fixed
     damage, PP, volatile effects, side conditions, weather, terrain and switching. Moves with
     unique rules get a "special" key that a handler in game.js reads. Only moves that need
     items, Tera or a doubles partner are marked unsupported and never put in a set;
   - picks 4 moves from the first role of Showdown's Gen 9 random-battle set: its best utility
     moves first (hazards, recovery, pivots, Protect...), then its strongest same-type and
     coverage attacks. Species with no random-battle set get strong same-type moves and coverage
     moves that hit the most new types super-effectively;
   - picks the first ability the engine implements, in this order: random-battle set abilities,
     then the species' normal abilities, then its hidden ability. This means some Pokémon don't
     get their signature ability; for example Darmanitan gets Sheer Force rather than Zen Mode;
   - picks a Hard-mode replacement for any setup move (its best unused attack);
   - tags legendaries ("L" = restricted legendary or mythical, "S" = sub-legendary, Paradox or
     Ultra Beast).
   - stores every species' weight (kg) for Low Kick / Grass Knot.
   Species #1 to #1025, plus 98 alternate forms (see D). Hand-curated entries always take priority
   over generated ones.
   Pokémon with almost no attacks (Ditto, Wobbuffet, Unown) get Return as a placeholder, and they
   are too weak to appear in the draft anyway.

C) Verification (tools/verify-data.js)
   Run "node tools/verify-data.js" after any data change. It checks every Pokémon's base stats,
   types, ability, moves (learnable, including from pre-evolutions) and Hard-mode swap move, and
   that every move exists in Showdown's data, that every alternate form has a PokeAPI sprite
   number, that no set contains a move the engine can't play,
   and that every move with special rules has a handler in game.js. It also checks every boss: a
   valid role, exactly 6 Pokémon that all exist, and unique keys. It exits with an error if
   anything doesn't match.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D) Alternate forms (dex-all.js, 98 of them)
   A form is included when Showdown lists it as a separate forme and its types or base stats differ
   from the base species. That covers every Alolan, Galarian, Hisuian and Paldean form (including
   the three Paldean Tauros breeds and the Galarian birds), Rotom's appliances, Deoxys, Wormadam,
   Shaymin-Sky, the Therian forms, Kyurem-Black/White, Zygarde-10%, Hoopa-Unbound, Oricorio,
   Lycanroc, Necrozma's fusions, Urshifu-Rapid-Strike, Calyrex's riders, Ursaluna-Bloodmoon, and
   the female Indeedee, Basculegion and Oinkologne.
   - Forms that normally need a held item have it built in: Dialga, Palkia and Giratina Origin
     (1.2x power on their two types), Ogerpon's masks (1.2x power, and Ivy Cudgel takes the mask's
     type), and Zacian- and Zamazenta-Crowned. Raging Bull takes each Paldean Tauros breed's type.
   - Names follow Showdown ("Ninetales-Alola", "Rotom-Wash"). Each form keeps its National Dex
     number (for the Shiny Dex and generation tabs) plus a separate sprite number from PokeAPI,
     which numbers forms from 10001 up (tools/showdown-data/pokeapi-forms.json).
   - Movesets, abilities and Hard-mode swaps are picked the same way as for other generated
     Pokémon. Forms with few moves of their own (Rotom's appliances, Oricorio...) also learn the
     base species' moves, as in Showdown.
   - The generator avoids moves that usually fail (Focus Punch without Substitute, Dream Eater and
     Snore without sleep).

8. WHERE THE TRAINER TEAMS COME FROM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There are 187 bosses, all in data.js (the BOSSES list). Each has a role: gym (Gym Leaders, Alola's
Island Kahunas and Trial Captains), e4 (Elite Four), champion, villain, or rival.

Who is included:
- Every Gym Leader, Elite Four member and Champion from Kanto to Paldea, plus the Blueberry League
  (Indigo Disk) and Alola's Trial Captains. A trainer with several roles (Koga, Bruno, Lance, Blue,
  Larry, Hala, Olivia, Acerola...) has one entry.
- Villain teams: bosses and their important members - Team Rocket (Giovanni, Archer, Ariana,
  Proton, Petrel), Magma (Maxie, Tabitha, Courtney), Aqua (Archie, Matt, Shelly), Galactic (Cyrus,
  Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Charon), Plasma (Ghetsis, N, Colress, Zinzolin), Flare (Lysandre, Xerosic,

How the teams were built:
- The ~125 trainers added in the full-roster and rival updates use their STRONGEST CANON TEAM from Bulbapedia:
  the highest-level team listed on each trainer's page (usually a rematch, the Pokémon World
  Tournament, Champion rematches or post-game battles). It was read automatically from the team
  tables in the built-in browser. Then:
  - everything is fully evolved (Nosepass -> Probopass, Golbat -> Crobat, ...);
  - alternate forms are kept (Alolan Persian, Galarian Darmanitan, Rotom-Heat...);
  - short teams are filled to 6 with Pokémon that fit the trainer's type or theme (for example
    most villain admins only had 2-3 Pokémon in the games).
  - The ace (signature or highest-level Pokémon) is last.
- Charon never battles in the games, so his team is themed on his Rotom and Heatran research.
- The original 60 bosses were written earlier and are inspired by the characters' best-known
  teams rather than read from Bulbapedia. For example Lance has Salamence.
- After alternate forms were added, every boss page was scanned again for forms (Bulbapedia's
  sprite file names include them, such as 0038Ninetales-Alola.png). 21 teams changed:
  - same slot, now the right form: Hau (Alolan Raichu), Sophocles (Alolan Golem), Kiawe (Alolan
    Marowak), Nanu (Alolan Persian), Molayne, Hapu and Amarys (Alolan Dugtrio), Plumeria (Alolan
    Muk), Opal (Galarian Weezing), Lacey (Galarian Slowbro), Larry (female Oinkologne), Colress
    (Rotom-Wash), Crispin and Lucas (Rotom-Heat);
  - Pokémon restored after being dropped for lack of their form: Kukui (Alolan Ninetales, instead
    of a second starter), Melony (Galarian Darmanitan), Bede (Galarian Rapidash), Faba (Alolan
    Raichu), Grusha (Alolan Ninetales), Olivia (Alolan Golem and Midnight Lycanroc), and Volo
    (Hisuian Arcanine, with Giratina now in its Origin Forme).
- Movesets are the same as everyone else's (section 7). Bosses don't get special sets.
- The quotes are ORIGINAL short lines written in each character's style, not lines from the games.
- Legendaries on boss teams: Zapdos and Moltres (Lt. Surge, Blaine), Groudon (Maxie), Kyogre
  (Archie), Darkrai (Cyrus), Giratina-Origin (Volo), Zekrom (N), Yveltal (Lysandre), Heatran (Charon),
  Zamazenta (Hop). On those days the draft pool may include one legendary (section 3).
- Trainer sprites come from Pokémon Showdown (Arven uses "arven-v"). Aliana and Celosia have none there, so they show a
  Poké Ball silhouette.
- The 👥 button in the header lists every boss and team by region, with filters for Gym Leader,
  Elite Four, Champion, Villain and Rival, and a Practice button on each.

9. IMAGES AND ANIMATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All images are hotlinked (loaded from the original sites, not stored in this folder):
- Pokémon sprites: PokéAPI's sprite repository
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokeAPI/sprites/master/sprites/pokemon/<dex#>.png
    and .../back/<dex#>.png for your side. Alternate forms use PokeAPI's form number instead
    (10001 and up). Newer Pokémon without a back sprite fall back to a
    mirrored front sprite.
- Trainer sprites: Pokémon Showdown, https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/sprites/trainers/<name>.png
  (a few bosses use a different file name, set by the "sprite" field in BOSSES).
- Move effect images: Pokémon Showdown, https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/fx/<name>.png

Move animations (anim.js) are written for this site and do NOT reuse Showdown's animation code.
Each move gets an animation chosen from its type, category and flags:
  - special moves: a projectile, or a beam for big (110+ power) and pulse moves;
  - physical moves: a lunge and impact, with a fist for punches, closing jaws for bites, a claw
    for slicing moves, and a quick dash for priority moves;
  - Ground moves shake the screen, Rock moves drop rocks, and sound moves send out rings;
  - status moves swirl around the target, and setup or healing moves make the user glow and
    sparkle;
  - stat changes show arrows, a new status shows a coloured pulse, and weather shows an overlay.
They speed up at 2×, are skipped at 4×, and can be turned off with the ✨ button.

10. SAVING, STATS, SHARING AND SHINY HUNTING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Everything is stored in the browser's localStorage under the key "dailyChampion.v1". Nothing
  is sent anywhere. Clearing site data, or using another browser or device, starts fresh.
- One official attempt per day. The chosen difficulty is locked once the battle starts.
- Progress is saved at the end of every turn, so closing the tab and coming back resumes the
  battle (any turn that was mid-way restarts from its beginning).
- Practice battles (the same boss with a new draft, a random boss, or any boss from the 👥 list)
  are never saved and never count toward stats.
- Stats: games played, win rate, current and best streak (consecutive days won), how many
  Pokémon were left on each win, wins by difficulty, and the last 20 results.
- Share text (copied to the clipboard) looks like:
    Daily Champion #3 🏆 vs Cynthia
    Hard · 24 turns · 3/6 left
    Me:   🟩🟩🟥🟩🟥🟥
    Boss: 💀💀💀💀💀💀
- Settings (also in localStorage): battle speed (1×/2×/4×) and animations on/off.

Shiny hunting and the Shiny Dex:
- When a new daily run is created, each of the 9 draft cards rolls a 1 in 100 chance (about 8.6%
  per day that at least one hits). On a hit, the player gets a BONUS 10th card: a shiny Pokémon
  picked at random from ALL 1,123 Pokémon (every species and alternate form). That includes weak and unevolved Pokémon and
  legendaries that never appear in the normal pool. The shared 9 cards stay the same for
  everyone; only the bonus card is personal.
- The roll uses normal randomness (not the date seed), so each player's luck is their own. It is
  saved with the day, so reloading never re-rolls. It never picks a Pokémon already in that day's
  pool, and it re-rolls up to 3 times to avoid ones already in your Shiny Dex.
- To collect it, put the shiny in your team of 6 and WIN the daily battle (it doesn't have to
  survive). It then goes into the Shiny Dex with the date, boss and difficulty. Lose, and it "got
  away". Drafting a weak shiny is the challenge: you have to win while carrying it.
- Shinies only appear in the official daily battle, never in practice.
- Shiny sprites come from PokéAPI (sprites/pokemon/shiny/<dex#>.png and back/shiny/...). A gold
  sparkle plays when a shiny is sent out.
- The Shiny Dex (📖 button, also linked from Stats and the result screen) shows all 1,123 Pokémon in
  dex order (each form right after its base species), with generation tabs and a "Collected only" filter. Collected ones show in shiny
  colours; the rest are silhouettes. It is saved in localStorage as "shinies".
- The result share text adds "✨ Shiny caught: <name>" when you catch one.

11. REBUILDING THE POKÉDEX, AND HOSTING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rebuild moves-gen.js and dex-all.js after changing abilities, the move converter or the Showdown data:
  1. Download the four Showdown files listed in section 7 into tools/showdown-data/
     (sets.json saved as sets9.json).
  2. node tools/build-dex.js
  3. node tools/verify-data.js   (should print "All checks passed.")
The build script reads data.js to know which moves, Pokémon and abilities already exist, so curated data
is never overwritten.

Running locally: any static file server works, for example
  python -m http.server 5174
then open http://localhost:5174. Opening index.html directly from disk also mostly works.

Hosting: it's a plain static site (HTML/CSS/JS), so GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages and
similar all work. Upload index.html, styles.css, moves-gen.js, data.js, dex-all.js, anim.js and game.js. The
tools/ folder is not needed online.
